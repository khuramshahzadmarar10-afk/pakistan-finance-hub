How compound interest grows money

With simple interest, you earn only on your original deposit. With compound interest, each interest credit is added to the balance, so the next period’s interest is calculated on the principal plus everything already earned. That snowball is why the growth curve bends upward over time instead of rising in a straight line. This calculator takes a starting principal, an annual interest rate, a term in years and a compounding frequency, then shows the maturity value, the interest earned and the effective growth percentage, with a year-by-year chart of the balance.

The formula it uses

The tool applies the standard compound interest formula: A = P × (1 + r ÷ m)m × t, where P is the principal, r is the annual rate, m is how many times interest compounds per year (1 for yearly, 2 for half-yearly, 4 for quarterly, 12 for monthly) and t is the term in years. Interest earned is simply A − P. Because the rate is divided by the compounding periods but applied more often, more frequent compounding produces a slightly higher result at the same headline rate.

A worked example

Deposit Rs 500,000 at 10% a year for 5 years, compounded monthly — the defaults above. The maturity value comes to roughly Rs 8.22 lakh, meaning about Rs 3.22 lakh of interest on your Rs 5 lakh principal, an effective growth of about 64%. Switch the frequency to yearly and the same deposit ends a little lower, because interest is credited once a year instead of twelve times. The gap looks small over five years; over twenty years it becomes one of the biggest free wins in saving.

Where this applies in Pakistan

Compounding is the engine inside National Savings schemes, bank term deposits, money-market and income funds, and the profit credited on many savings accounts — although each product has its own crediting rules, minimum balances and withholding tax on profit. Use this calculator to compare a deposit offer against a monthly investing habit: a lump sum belongs here, while regular monthly amounts belong in the SIP calculator. Keep in mind the result assumes a constant nominal rate with no deposits, withdrawals, taxes or fees, so treat it as a clean comparison of scenarios rather than a promise from any institution.

Compound Interest FAQs

These calculators are planning aids. Financial products, tax filings and market transactions may include details a simple estimate cannot capture.

How does compound interest work?

Compound interest adds earned returns to the balance. Later returns are then calculated on both the starting principal and prior growth, which can accelerate growth over longer periods.

Does compounding frequency affect the result?

At the same nominal annual rate, more frequent compounding usually produces a slightly higher result because returns are credited sooner. Compare yearly, half-yearly, quarterly and monthly options in the calculator.

What data leaves my browser?
